## Section 1

Angel almost turned around in the parking lot.

Not because anything was wrong — the opposite, if anything. The building
was fine, unremarkable from the outside, the kind of address that could
have belonged to any of a dozen companies. It was just further from home
than she was used to driving for a job, and driving further for something
unfamiliar had a way of making her question herself before she'd even
gotten out of the car.

She'd been a web developer for a couple of years by then, but always
in-house — one company, one product, one set of expectations she'd long
since learned to meet without thinking too hard about it. She didn't
really know what a digital agency *was*, not really, not in practice. She
just knew this one paid better and sounded more interesting than doing the
same maintenance work for the same company for another year.

She was married. She had a small daughter at home who took up most of the
space in her chest most days. She'd worn a suit to the interview — the
version of herself she knew how to be in a room like this, put-together,
professional, a woman who'd shown up to plenty of interviews before and
knew exactly what that required of her. Even on an ordinary day at her old
jobs, the most casual she ever got was a polo shirt and dress jeans — what
everyone there just called "weekend clothes," said like it was a category
that needed its own name, because actual weekend clothes never once made
it through the door.

The room they put her in didn't look like any office she'd sat in before.
Nothing about it looked like it was trying to be taken seriously in the
way her old jobs had been serious — no framed corporate mission statement,
no matching furniture bought in one bulk order. It looked like a place
where people actually wanted to be, which was a strange thing for a
building to communicate to her before anyone had even walked in.

Then James walked in, and Angel understood the room a little better.

He wasn't wearing a suit. He wasn't wearing anything close to a suit —
jeans, a t-shirt, a laptop tucked under one arm like he'd just wandered
over from his own desk rather than prepared for an interview at all. No
jacket. No effort to look like the occasion required more of him than a
Tuesday did.

It landed on Angel like a small, quiet culture shock. Her whole
professional life up to that point had run on a specific, unspoken rule —
dress like the job matters, dress like you take it seriously, dress like
someone might judge you for getting it wrong. James walked in like none of
that had ever occurred to him, entirely at ease in a way that had nothing
to do with not caring and everything to do with simply not needing the
costume.

She didn't know yet what to make of him. She just knew, sitting there in
her suit, that she'd walked into a version of this industry she hadn't
known existed.
